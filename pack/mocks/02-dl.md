## A. Conceptual (15 min)

### A1. When DL loses

State four concrete reasons a 12-layer Transformer on lagged FX returns will underperform LightGBM on the same tabular features.

### A2. Receptive field vs horizon

You predict a 10-second return with a causal TCN. How do kernel size, dilation, and depth set the **lookback**? What goes wrong if lookback includes a **fixing** that will not have printed at t in live trading?

### A3. Normalisation

Why is **global** batch-norm dangerous on a stream of ticks? What would you use instead for a live eFX net?

### A4. Attention is not causality

### A5. Overfitting the microstructure

### A6. Deep hedging in one minute

What problem does deep hedging solve that delta-hedging a Black–Scholes book does not? Name one **risk-measure** you would train, and one reason **Risk** will still want Greeks.

## Model answers

### A1

(1) **Small effective N**: years of ticks are not i.i.d. samples; a net’s capacity fits **seasonality and one-off events**. (2) **Shift**: the test month is not the train month; trees + a few economically named features degrade more gracefully. (3) **Loss vs PnL**: CE on sign ≠ markout; DL will exploit **label artefacts**. (4) **Latency and ops**: 12-layer attention is the wrong serving shape; distillation adds another failure mode. (5) **Tuning surface**: DL has more knobs, so more **selection bias**. (6) **Missingness and regime**: trees split on “feature missing”; nets need imputed sentinels you will get wrong.

### A2

Causal TCN lookback ≈ 1 + (k−1) * Σ dilation, for typical exponential dilations ~ 2^{depth}. If depth/dilation reaches **through the unprinted fix**, you trained on a feature that is **future information** relative to live. Also: if the label horizon is 10s but the net sees 30 minutes of **overlapping** path, it memorizes local trajectories. Match **receptive field to economically available information**, and **cut sequences on events**.

### A3

Batch-norm uses **batch statistics** that (a) differ between train mini-batches of mixed sessions and (b) are **not available** the same way online (one tick is not a batch). It also **leaks** via population stats if you update them carelessly. Prefer: **frozen** per-feature running moments computed **point-in-time**, LayerNorm / RMSNorm on the channel dim, or **online standardisation** with slow EMA that is itself a **controlled parameter**. Never update BN population stats from live in an unapproved way.

### A4

Attention is a **weighted average in a fitted basis**, not a causal effect. It is unstable under multicollinearity (USDJPY and Nikkei futures). Use it as **debugging**, not as an explanation to a client or to Compliance. If you need attribution, prefer **interventions** (ablate the feed) or simple ablations, and still speak in **predictive** language.

### A5

The CNN learned **engine artefacts** (queue priority, lot size, matching rules, feed snapshots) that **vanished** in the upgrade — classic **overfit to venue microstructure**. Also possible: **label leakage** from snapshots that include the event. Production response: freeze, **kill**, rebuild features from **spec of the new engine**, shorter memory, simpler model, and a **canary on a tiny order-flow slice**. This is why DL on LOB needs **venue-change tests** in CI.

### A6

Deep hedging trains a **policy** that minimises a **risk measure of terminal P&L** under **frictions** (spread, impact, discrete time, constraints) where continuous delta in a complete market is a fantasy. Typical: **CVaR**, expected shortfall, or exponential utility. Risk will still want **Greeks, scenario ladders, and limits** because the policy is only as good as the **simulated measure**; they need a language for **limit breaches** that is not “the net wanted to”.

### B1 — recommendation

**90-day: classical.** Queue position, time-in-force, spread, imbalance at top K, time-of-day, own-order flags — logistic or GBDT, served in the **algo box in HK co-lo**. LOB images: research track with 1 scientist, **not** on the live router. DL becomes interesting when you have **stable, reconstructed book** and a **latency budget in milliseconds not microseconds**. MRM: image nets are a **harder** explain story for best-execution. Tell the VP: papers ≠ HKEX production.

### B2

Imitation: copies **old** mistakes and yesterday’s risk appetite; fine as a **baseline**. Offline RL: need **logged policies and propensities**; KRW NDF is **thin** — sim-to-real will dominate. Hawkes + inventory: **few parameters**, trader-interpretable, easier SS1/23. Path: **Hawkes/inventory in prod**, imitation as shadow, RL **only in simulator** until the logging stack exists. Conduct: algos that **detect** client type from flow can become **predatory**; keep the objective **implementation shortfall + risk**, not “pick off the client”.

### B3

DeepBSDE / operator nets: **research** for exotic/RR calibration speed, not a Q3 desk widget. Productionise first: **arb-free surface interpolators**, PCA/autoencoder on tenors with **hard calendar/butterfly constraints**, and a **fast pricer**. A net that **violates no-arb** is a non-starter. Use DL as a **warm start** to a classical calibrator.

### B4

Start with **count / FM** on products × clients using **the e-trading platform and RFQ logs**. Two-tower when you have enough events. LLM on chat: **useful for intent**, dangerous as the **only** embedding (PII, off-channel, jokes). Never train on **other clients’ confidential flow** to predict this client. Separate **research embeddings** from **production embeddings**. Reset embeddings on coverage-change. multi-stakeholder bonus: **multi-objective** ranking (client utility, desk risk, coverage fairness).

### C1 — the desk assistant

**Diagram:** user utterance → **intent classifier / LLM with grammar** → **tools**: `get_mid(pair)`, `get_skew`, `get_limits`, `draft_ticket` → **confirmation UI** → trader or client ack → EMS.

LLM **must not** invent a mid. It may **phrase** a mid returned by the pricer. RAG: **product docs, playbooks, client-specific *permitted* static data** — **not** other clients’ tickets, **not** unscoped chat.

Fine-tune: only if logs are **clean and consented**; otherwise RAG + tools + small intent model.

HKMA: customer-facing GenAI → **human-in-the-loop** at least at this stage; define **scope** in policy (initiate vs price vs commit).

Telemetry: prompt, retrieved docs IDs, tool I/O, user, client, timestamp, model version, whether price was **overridden**. Retention per policy.

Tier: **workflow / assistant**, not a pricing model — **unless** you let it commit risk, in which case it **becomes** a trading model. That sentence is the interview.

### C2 — deep hedging

Simulator must include **spread, discrete hedge times, APAC overnight jump, funding**. Objective: **CVaR_0.05 of discounted P&L** plus a **transaction penalty**. Train off policy in PyTorch; **never** live.

Say **no** if: (i) they cannot **replicate** current desk hedges as a baseline, (ii) no **limit** layer, (iii) they want to replace the engine before shadowing a **single tenor**, (iv) the book is **client-facing vanilla** where explaining a net hedge to a corporate is a conduct problem.

Shadow: same fills, compare **hedge error and cost** vs delta engine for 1w ATM only. Promote per tenor.
