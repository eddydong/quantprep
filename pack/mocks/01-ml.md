## A. Rapid-fire (20 min)

Answer in four sentences or fewer. Then check the key.

### A1. Leakage

A researcher wants to predict 5-minute USDCNH mid returns with features that include the **same-window realised volatility** computed from the **same mid series**, plus a **session VWAP that includes the current bar**. What is wrong, and what do you do instead?

### A2. Why trees still win

Why do gradient-boosted trees still beat deep nets on **tabular eFX features** (spreads, OFI, inventory, time-of-day, markout lags)? When would you override that default?

### A3. The metric trap

A VP shows you AUC 0.62 on predicting “client will lift our offer in the next 50ms”. Why might this model **lose money**, and which metric would you put on the dashboard for the trader?

### A4. Non-stationarity

Name three distinct non-stationarities in APAC FX and a practical ML response to each (not “retrain often”).

### A5. Calibration vs ranking

A toxicity classifier is well-ranked (AUC 0.71) but uncalibrated. Why does that break **last-look or spread-widen** policies, and how do you fix it **online**?

### A6. Regularisation as risk

L2 vs dropout vs early stopping vs feature bagging: which one is a **risk control** in a markets setting, and which is merely a training trick?

### A7. Causal vs predictive

Sales wants a model of “what if we showed this client a tighter spread?” You have observational RFQ data. What identification problem is this, and what is an honest ML substitute if you cannot randomise?

### A8. Multiple testing

A QR tests 400 features on two years of EURUSD and keeps those with t>2. What do you require before this set enters a production candidate?

### A9. Class imbalance

Toxic flow is 2% of trades. Accuracy is 98%. What do you do to the **loss**, the **sampler**, and the **decision threshold** — three different knobs?

### A10. Ensemble of experts

Would you train **one global FX model** or **per-pair specialists** (USDCNH, USDJPY, USDKRW NDF)? Give a hierarchical alternative.

## Model answers (fold after you attempt)

### A1

Same-window realised vol uses the return you are predicting if the window includes t. Session VWAP including the current bar is **direct leakage**. Use **strictly lagged** realised measures, decaying kernels that stop at t−1, and VWAP as-of the previous bar. If you need contemporaneous vol, it must come from **other instruments** or a **leading** microstructure channel that will exist at decision time (e.g. lagged futures). Document the **information set** I_{t−}.

### A2

Ticks → sparse, mixed-type, heavy-tailed, missing, strong **known interactions** (tod × pair × inventory) that trees partition cheaply. Nets need more data, careful normalisation, and still lose on **shift**. Override when the raw input is a **tensor** (LOB snapshots, vol surface, text) or you need a **policy** (hedging) not a score. Even then: DL encoder + tree/linear head is often the grown-up design.

### A3

AUC ignores **asymmetric cost**, **size**, **inventory**, and **adverse selection after the fill**. A high-AUC model can flag retail flow as “easy” and **tighten**, then get picked off by a last-look predator that is rare but large. Dashboard: **markout at 50ms/1s/10s by bucket**, fill ratio, internalization quality, and **PnL per million** vs control. Use a **cost-sensitive** threshold.

### A4

Examples: (1) **Session liquidity** (Tokyo lunch) → time-of-day features + **stateful** spread multiplier, not a global residual. (2) **Regime/vol** (PBOC, US-China headlines) → regime detector, widen or fall back to a robust linear mid. (3) **Fixing/event** (WMR, CNY fix) → **calendar features and model off** around events. (4) **Balance-sheet / quarter-end** → inventory penalty schedule. Retraining is not a response; **fallback policies** are.

### A5

Policies need **P(toxic | x)**, not a rank. If 0.2 means 0.8 in the tail, you will **under-widen** on the dangerous 2%. Fix: Platt or isotonic **on a purged holdout**, then **online temperature / logistic intercept** with a slow learning rate, monitored against realised markouts. Recalibrate **per pair and session**. Never isotonic-fit on the trading day itself without regularisation.

### A6

**Early stopping on a purged validation set** and **feature/model freeze with dual-run** are risk controls. Dropout is a training trick that disappears at eval (unless MC-dropout, which you probably will not run at 50µs). L2 is a prior; useful, not a control. **Bagging / CPCV distribution of Sharpe** is a control because it estimates **selection bias**.

### A7

Confounding: you showed tight spreads to **clients you already believed were uninformed**, or when **inventory wanted the trade**. That is not the causal effect of tightening. Honest substitutes: (1) **logged propensity** of historical quoting policy and IPS; (2) **discontinuity** at automated spread grids; (3) **shadow quotes** / small sanctioned exploration with a bandit **cap**; (4) predict **outcome conditional on spread** as a **forecast**, labelled as such, not a treatment effect. Do not say “we used SHAP so it is causal”.

### A8

Require: pre-registered hypothesis or **train-only** discovery; **purged** multiple-testing (e.g. deflated Sharpe, CPCV); stability across years/pairs; **economic** cap on feature count; a **hold-out pair or hold-out year** never used for selection. 400 tests with t>2 is the definition of a false Sharpe.

### A9

Loss: **weighted logistic** or focal, weights = cost of miss vs false widen. Sampler: **do not** naively downsample the easy majority if those trades **pay the bills**; keep them but down-weight. Threshold: set on **expected markout**, not F1. Three knobs, three meetings (QR, trader, MRM).

### A10

Hierarchical: **global backbone** (tod, session, cross-asset vol, USD index) + **pair-specific residual** (CNH fixing, KRW NDF hours). Complete pooling fails on CNH; no pooling fails on data. Hierarchical Bayesian or a **shared GBDT with pair ID + pair×feature interactions**. Specialists for top pairs, pooled tail pairs.

### B1 — sketch they want

- **Decision time** t, label = signed move / toxic fill over horizon h (e.g. 1s, 10s). Overlap ⇒ **purge** any train sample whose time is within h of a test sample (Lopez de Prado embargo).
- Splits: **walk-forward by week**, not random rows. Embargo around **events**.
- **CPCV**: many combinatorial holdouts to get a **distribution** of PnL, not one lucky year.
- Session: evaluate **Tokyo, HK afternoon, London CNH, NY** separately. A model that only works in London is a London model.
- Costs: apply **typical half-spread + expected widen** in the backtest. A classifier that always says “toxic” has great recall and zero franchise.
- Go-live: **shadow for 4–6 weeks**, dual-run vs current skew, kill if markout or fill-ratio breach. Model **tier** = high if it auto-skews.

### B2 — sketch they want

- Clocks: exchange timestamps, not notebook `datetime.now()`.
- Point-in-time: as-of join; no “last” without `asof`.
- Two compute paths: **slow** (SageMaker, research, minutes) vs **hot** (C++/Java/kdb on box next to the pricing engine — Singapore). HK team owns **spec and research**; engine owners own **hot implementation**. Same unit tests, golden ticks.
- Version features as **hashes**; model binary pins a feature hash.
- Do not put a Python GBDT on the 50µs path; **distil** to a linear/spline/small net or a lookup.

### B3 — sketch they want

- Stakeholders: client (execution quality, fairness), salesperson (wallet, coverage), trader (inventory, franchise, conduct). CTR maximisation **will** dump risk into the book.
- Implicit feedback: RFQs, chat intents, the e-trading platform clicks; position bias.
- Start with **contextual bandit / two-tower** on client embedding × product embedding, **constrained** by a risk layer (hard inventory limits). Kernel FM is a fair adjacent answer if you can explain it.
- Conduct: monitor **price quality and reject rates by client cluster**; no protected-class proxies; audit logs.
- Offline: inverse-propensity on historical display policy; if logging is missing, **you cannot evaluate** — that is the first engineering ticket.

### B4 — sketch they want

- Intraday: **intercept / temperature / EWMA residual** of a **frozen** approved model is often acceptable; full tree retraining is a **model change**.
- Agree with MRM **what is a parameter update vs a new model**.
- Bandits: exploration is a **client outcome**; cap ε, exclude sensitive client classes, log everything.
- Fallback: if live Brier/markout blows up, **revert to the approved frozen model**.

### C — a strong 30-minute answer (outline)

**PnL language:** reduce **adverse markouts** on USDCNH and USDJPY Asia lunch without dropping **good fill rate** more than X bps of franchise.

**Label:** fill-level markout at 1s and 10s, plus a binary toxic flag at a quantile; horizon matches how fast the desk can hedge.

**Features (example):** lagged OFI, spread vs session quantile, inventory, time-to-fix, CNH-CNY basis, USDJPY futures lead, residual vs global mid, last N own-fills’ markouts, venue mix, realised vol **lagged**.

**Model:** LightGBM or L2 logistic on standardised features; **not** a transformer. Distil for hot path.

**Validation:** purged walk-forward, CPCV, session slices, costed PnL.

**Shadow:** identical quotes in a paper book; trader override logged; weekly review.

**Staff:** you + 1 modeller + 1 data/quant dev; London MLOps for registry; Singapore eFX for hot path.

**30/60/90:** feature PIT audit; shadow live; go-live on one pair one session with kill switch.
